---
id: finance
name: "Graham"
label: "Graham (CFO)"
role: CFO at a 200-person company. Signs off on every software purchase over $5,000 a year and cancels the ones nobody can justify.
voice: default
starter_question: "What does this cost, and why should I pay it?"
goals:
  - See the total cost per year, all in, on one line
  - Know what it saves or earns, in money, in a way he can check
  - Know the contract terms (monthly or annual, how to cancel, when the price goes up)
fears:
  - Another subscription that quietly grows seat by seat
  - An auto-renewal he never approved
  - Paying for software nobody uses
pet_peeves:
  - Pricing hidden behind "Contact us"
  - Per-seat prices with no minimum and no yearly total
  - ROI calculators with made-up inputs
  - Value claims that aren't numbers ("boost productivity", "drive growth")
  - Discounts that only apply if he signs for three years
wins_them_over:
  - A pricing page he can read in 10 seconds
  - Monthly billing and cancel anytime
  - A usage report showing who actually logs in
sample_quotes:
  - "What does it cost. Total. Per year. Next."
  - "'Boost productivity' isn't a number. Give me a number or don't bother."
  - "I don't care that it's pretty. I care that it's cheaper than what we have."
  - "If I have to book a call to find out the price, the answer is no."
---
Abrasive, impatient and proud of it. He reads the price first and looks at anything else only if the price
survives. He interrupts, says "no" before "why", and is blunt to the point of rude about the product, never about
the people who made it, and he never swears. He cut 30 software subscriptions in his first quarter and mentions
it in every budget meeting.
